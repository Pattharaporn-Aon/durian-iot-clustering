# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This repository accompanies a peer-reviewed research article, and we want taking part
in it — asking a question, reporting a problem, proposing a change, or reusing the
pipeline — to be a straightforward and respectful experience for everyone, regardless
of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics,
gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Expected behaviour

- Be respectful and considerate in issues, pull requests and code review.
- Assume good faith. Many participants are researchers rather than software engineers,
  and questions that seem basic are welcome.
- Give and accept constructive criticism about the *work*, not the person.
- Raise disagreements about method or results with evidence, and be willing to be
  shown wrong.
- Respect the scientific record: do not misrepresent the data, the code, or another
  person's contribution.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or derogatory comments, public or private.
- Discriminatory language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Personal or political attacks, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Publishing another person's private information without explicit permission.
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional or academic setting.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces — issues, pull requests,
commits, discussions and the repository's documentation — and whenever an individual
is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness behaviour that breaches this Code of Conduct, please
report it to:

**Pattharaporn Thongnim** — Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Science,
Burapha University, Chon Buri, Thailand — <pattharaporn@buu.ac.th>

All reports will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. The privacy and
safety of the reporter will be respected. If a report concerns the maintainer, it may
instead be raised with the Faculty of Science, Burapha University, or with the
journal handling the associated article.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards and may take
any action they judge appropriate and proportionate, including:

1. **Correction** — a private, written explanation of why the behaviour was
   inappropriate.
2. **Warning** — a warning with consequences for continued behaviour.
3. **Temporary restriction** — a temporary ban from interaction with the project.
4. **Permanent ban** — a permanent ban from any interaction with the project.

Maintainers may also edit, remove or reject comments, commits, code, issues and other
contributions that do not align with this Code of Conduct, giving reasons where
appropriate.
